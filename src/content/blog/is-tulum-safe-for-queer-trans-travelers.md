Yes — Tulum is one of the most welcoming places in Mexico for queer and trans travelers, and I say that as someone who has lived here for ten years. But "safe" deserves an honest, specific answer, not a brochure one. Here's what I actually tell friends before they come.

## Is Tulum queer and trans friendly?

Tulum isn't a queer-centric town — there's no gayborhood, no single famous gay bar. But that's not the same as being unwelcoming. You'll see queer and gender-diverse people any time you walk out on the street. We're a regular part of the community and of daily life here, woven into the town rather than concentrated in one corner of it.

Because Tulum is so international, acceptance tends to come naturally. People from all over the world live and visit here, and that diversity does a lot of quiet work.

### Are there queer bars or events in Tulum?
There's no dedicated "gayborhood" here — Tulum's queer scene is woven into daily life rather than concentrated in one area. Red Room exists as a bar, though it leans specifically toward gay men rather than the broader queer community. ### Is Tulum dangerous in general, aside from LGBTQ+ safety?
The road between the town center and the beach zone has no lighting or sidewalk and isn't safe to walk at night — take a taxi or colectivo after dark. This applies to everyone, not just queer and trans travelers.
